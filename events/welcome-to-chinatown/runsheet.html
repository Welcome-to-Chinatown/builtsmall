9:453 minSlides 15–17

Filters → audit steps → the one-liner

  • Three filters: repetitive, text or data, describable.
  • Audit: pick a task → describe it step by step → time it → name the PITA.
  • Problem statement: "My pain point is that ___. If I had ___, I would ___."

Exercise · Find your PITA (pairs)

  1. 2 min — on your own, fill the first steps on the PITA Canvas.
  2. 3 min — pair up. One is the owner, one is the detective (asks, doesn't solve).
  3. 2 min — switch roles.
  4. 1 min — write the one-liner.
  5. 2 min — a few say their PITA out loud.
Share: "Two or three of you — what's your PITA?" Pick voices from different corners.

Exercise · Build the dashboard

  • Open the handout, download the sample file, attach to a new chat.
  • Fill the four-part template — they write the Steps.
  • Then make one change: add a feature, change a color, add a filter.
Check: revenue ~$3,825 · 100 orders · avg ~$38 · refunds $321.52 · 6 categories.
Share: "Who got 6 categories? Who got more?" Wrong numbers = a cleaning step got skipped.
How to teach the Steps without giving them away: tell them to look at the file first — the dates, the category names, the refunds. Each mess they spot becomes a step. Model one ("dates are inconsistent → Step 1: read all the date styles"), then let them find the rest.

Exercise · Now write your own

  • Pick a task — your PITA, or anything.
  • The hack: in a new chat, paste the four-part framework and say "interview me about this task, then write the prompt for me."
  • Answer its questions → it hands back a finished prompt → paste into a fresh chat and run.
If tight: "You've got the hack — start it here, finish tonight." Then move on.
