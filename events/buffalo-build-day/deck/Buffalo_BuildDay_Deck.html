Session 1

Responsible AI, then prompting

The ground rules, the words you will hear, and your first build.

Responsible AI

You're in control

Adopt AI in the way that is right for you.
These guardrails keep you safe. Do not let them stop you from starting.
Responsible AI

Our ground rules

RuleWhat it means
01Keep a human in the loopAI drafts, you decide. Never let it send money, sign, hire, or post on its own.
02Keep private things outNo card or account numbers, passwords, SSNs, or customer and employee records in public AI tools.
03Assume free tools may learnFree tools can train on what you type. Turn off training, or use a business plan for sensitive work.
04Customize for your communityOut-of-the-box AI doesn't reflect every culture or experience — adapt it to truly serve your audience.
Use fake or sample information only today.
Protect your data

Turn off training

Most chat assistants can stop training on what you type. Here is where to find the setting.
ChatGPT
Settings › Data controls › turn off “Improve the model for everyone.”
Claude
Settings › Privacy › turn off “Help improve our AI models.”
Gemini
Settings › Gemini Apps Activity › turn it Off.
Responsible AI

Before you paste or upload

Ask yourself: would I be okay sending this to an outside company?
  • Check your privacy settings and your organization's policy.
  • Keep confidential info out of unapproved tools.
  • Use fake, sample, or approved info for today.
Private settings alone don't make a tool safe. Approval and the provider's data practices matter too.

Prompting

What makes a good prompt

PartWhat it asksFor example
Role & goalWho should AI be, and what are you getting done?“You are my bookkeeper. Help me see how my shop is doing.”
StepsWhat should it do, step by step?“Here is my sales data. Add up revenue by category, then show the trend.”
OutputWhat should the result look like?“Build it as an interactive dashboard I can open in my browser.”
EvaluationHow will you know it is any good?“I should see my best sellers at a glance. Flag anything odd.”

Your first build

Write it with the four parts

Start from this — fill the blanksRole & goal: You are my data analyst. Help me understand my shop / nonprofit / practice's money. Steps: Here is my data (attach your file). 1. Clean it up — fix dates, merge duplicate names, trim stray spaces. 2. Break the totals down by  what matters to you . 3. Show how they change over time. Output: Build a visual, interactive dashboard — number cards, a bar chart, a table, and any charts that fit my data. Add how you want it to look and feel. Eval: Check the totals against my file, tell me the cleaning choices you made, and flag anything that looks off.
Fill step 2 — break the totals down by…

Businessproduct · category · day of week
Servicesclient · project · billable

Session 2

Find the task worth building

Start with the problem, not the technology. Then map how it really works.

Find it

Start with a PITA

What is the thing you do at 10pm on a Friday that you dread?
PITA = Pain In The Ass. The repeating task that drains your time, focus, or energy. The end-of-quarter scramble. The report you redo every month.
If you run a nonprofit
Sound familiar?
Intake forms. Funder reports. The same client questions. Tracking volunteers.
If you run a business
Sound familiar?
Customer inquiries. Reconciling sales. Repeat questions. Staff schedules.
We start with the problem, not the tool. Get that right and the build is easy.
Find it

Is it a good task for AI?

01
Steps
Can you write down the steps?
02
Data or text
Is it about words or information?
03
Repetitive
Does it repeat in the same shape?
04
Risk
Low risk? Let AI run. High risk? AI drafts, you check.
A strong first task has clear steps, runs on text or data, repeats often, and is low risk. Low-risk is the place to start.
A real PITA

Meet Jason, from Soft Swerve

Six hours a month, lost to tip wages.
Jason runs six ice cream shops. He wouldn't pay for enterprise QuickBooks, so every month he reconciled each store's books by hand just to figure out tip wages. Six hours, gone.
He found the one painful, repeating task and built for that. Now it takes five minutes.
He didn't start with AI. He started with the problem.
Find it

From a big idea to a buildable task

Too vague
Hard to build
"I want a tool that shows me all my sales and finances across my six stores."
Buildable
One task, one win
"Every month I spend hours copying sales and expenses from each store into a spreadsheet. If a dashboard pulled them together, I would see how all six stores are doing at a glance."
A buildable task names the work, the pain, and what good looks like.

Zoom in

One node, five parts

PartWhat it isFor the toast
TriggerWhat starts this step.The toaster dings.
ActorWho, or what, does it.You.
InputsWhat they need to do it.Is it the right color? Need longer?
ActionWhat actually happens.Take it out, butter it.
SuccessWhat a good result looks like.Golden, warm, buttered.

Human to AI

The same five parts, for AI

Node partFor an AI step
TriggerWhat kicks it off. You open the tool, or a new item lands.
ActorThe AI.
InputsThe context and data it needs. Your role and goal.
ActionThe steps you write into your prompt.
SuccessThe output, and how you know it is good enough.
The node map is your prompt plan. You still review before anything goes out.
Your turn · 60 minutes

Map your workflow

Map how your PITA works today.
  • In a text editor, write every step — the real way it happens now.
  • Draw it as nodes: each step is a box.
  • Circle the one node AI could take off your plate.
  • For that node, write the five parts: trigger, actor, inputs, action, success.
Start with how it actually works, not how AI might do it. That comes next.

Build it

Your four-part prompt

Copy this shapeRole & goal: You are helping me [do a specific task] for [your org or business]. Steps: Here is what you need to know: [context]. Do these steps: 1. [first step] 2. [second step] 3. [third step] Output: Return it as [format], with [what matters]. Eval: Check that [criteria] are met. Flag anything missing or that needs a human.
Fill it in from your node map and the target you just set.
Build it

Make it a reusable Project

Want it to last past today? Save it as a Project.
  • Make a Project or saved workspace (a Claude Project, a ChatGPT Project, or a Gemini Gem).
  • Give it a clear name.
  • Paste your best prompt as the instructions.
  • Add reference files — only if approved to share.
  • Test it with a fresh example.
Follow along. A Project means you don't rewrite the prompt every time.
Build Lab · 35 minutes

Build, run, improve

Make it work.
  • Write your first prompt (or set up your Project).
  • Run it and read the result.
  • Spot what it got wrong or missed — against your target.
  • Change your instructions and run again.
  • Repeat until it does the task.
Stuck? Grab a technologist. One change at a time works best.

Session 5

Test it, then make it better

Run it against reality, find the cracks, and refine toward your target.

Test it

Testing takes time

Test at home
Try it yourself
Run it and find the errors. About 2 weeks.
Test in the field
Pilot for real
Small, real cases. About 1 to 3 months.
Maintain
Keep improving
Collect feedback and fix. 3 to 12 months to production.
Jason didn't build his dashboard in a day. He built it in pieces and tested constantly. Today you build a prototype — real use comes after real testing.
Keep building · 45 minutes

Test it, then make it better

Try to break it, then fix it.
  • Run a normal case, then a messy one: missing info, odd formatting.
  • Run a case where it should stop and ask a human.
  • Compare each to the target you set before building.
  • Pick one problem. Add context or an example; say when a human reviews.
  • One change at a time. Run it again, save the better version.
